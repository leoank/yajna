Whole-file storage (what git-annex keeps) is 1.000 everywhere: no two files were identical.
Chunking and hashing ran at 930 to 1000 MB/s single-threaded with our crate and 1160 to
1300 MB/s with xet-core's (its gear hash is SIMD and skips more).

What it says:
- Dedup pays where versions share bytes: appended rows (1% added for 1% of rows), local
  edits (0.5% for three edits in 100 MB), repeated regions in a time-lapse (half the frames'
  chunks recur). It pays nothing for successive checkpoints of full training, a CSV that
  grows a column on every line each day, scattered row deletions (each touches a chunk), or
  a second sample.
- Compression matters as much as dedup, and LZ4 is weak: on sparse microscopy images zstd
  of each whole file keeps 6%, Xet's xorbs 32%; on the daily CSVs 35% against 64%. The Xet
  format allows only LZ4 and BG4-LZ4.
- BG4 helped BERT's weights (0.920 against 0.978 with LZ4) but not Pythia's (fp16,
  near-random mantissas); the automatic choice never did worse than LZ4.
- Our chunker is 20 to 25% slower than xet-core's, well above the 300 MB/s it needs.

Not covered: Parquet (no writer in the dev shell), lab imaging at TB scale, oppy.
