## Notes
Asked by ank on 2026-11-06 after [[RUN-20261106-4vftn2]]: keep LZ4 for compatibility, with
zstd as an option. Is zstd worth it at the scale a xorb compresses, one 64 KB chunk at a
time? On the same data (`zstd.tsv`, fractions of the input; the imaging sets together, the
CSV set its first version only):

| set | zstd -3 whole files | chunks, LZ4/BG4 | chunks, zstd -3 | chunks, zstd -19 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pythia-14m, early | 0.920 | 1.000 | 0.920 | 0.921 |
| Pythia-14m, late | 0.921 | 1.000 | 0.922 | 0.923 |
| BERT-tiny fine-tunes | 0.925 | 0.919 | 0.904 | 0.905 |
| imaging, both samples | 0.183 | 0.431 | 0.254 | 0.216 |
| JHU daily CSVs | 0.353 | 0.641 | 0.339 | 0.273 |
| EV CSV | 0.108 | 0.232 | 0.152 | 0.125 |

zstd -3 per chunk keeps most of what whole-file zstd gets (imaging 0.25 against 0.18, from
LZ4's 0.43) and, with dedup, beats it on the CSV versions; -19 adds a little more at a large
cost in time. zstd -3 compressed chunks at 350 to 1300 MB/s on rogue. So the option is
zstd -3 per chunk.
